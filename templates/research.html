Research

Many problems in optimization, equilibrium and data science can be written as a search for a point that an operator leaves unchanged. My research asks when such a point exists, what it says about the operator, and how to reach it with methods that are provably convergent and fast in practice.

The common thread

A great many questions reduce to finding \(x^\ast\) with

\[ x^\ast = T x^\ast \quad\text{or}\quad 0 \in A x^\ast + B x^\ast, \]

where \(T\) is a nonlinear map and \(A, B\) are monotone operators. Simple iterations such as \(x_{n+1} = T x_n\) may fail to converge, even for nonexpansive \(T\). Averaging repairs this: the Krasnoselskii–Mann scheme \(x_{n+1} = (1-\lambda)x_n + \lambda T x_n\) converges under mild conditions, and enriched mappings, a class I have studied extensively with Vasile Berinde and colleagues, are precisely those that averaging turns into tractable maps. The examples page lets you watch this happen.

Much of my work carries these ideas into spaces without linear structure, such as CAT(0) spaces, Hadamard manifolds and hyperbolic spaces, where convex combinations become geodesics and familiar inequalities must be rebuilt from the geometry.

Artificial intelligence for hard problems

A growing direction of my work brings machine learning and artificial intelligence into the design and analysis of methods for difficult problems in optimization and nonlinear analysis, and, in the other direction, uses optimization theory to train models such as extreme learning machines more reliably.
